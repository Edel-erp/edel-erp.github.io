What Edel does for import & export

An importer who prices goods on the invoice price alone is often selling at a loss once duty, surtax, clearing and transport are added. Edel builds the landed cost of every shipment line by line, and keeps your books in Birr and in foreign currency.

  1. Landed cost per shipmentFreight, insurance, duty, excise, VAT, surtax and withholding added to every load.
  2. Import taxes in the right orderEach tax worked out in Ethiopia's order, on top of the one before.
  3. Letters of creditFollow each LC from opening to settlement, with fees and expiry alerts.
  4. Foreign currency controlTrack foreign currency balances and rates, so imports match the currency you hold.
  5. Djibouti transitFollow each container from the port to clearance, with the bill of lading attached.
  6. Stock by shipmentKnow what each shipment brought in, at what cost, and where it is stored.
  7. Export payment follow-upSee every export payment and get warned before it is overdue.
  8. Books in Birr and foreign currencyExchange gains and losses shown per shipment.

Questions

How does Edel work out landed cost?

It adds freight and insurance to the goods value, then customs duty, excise, VAT, surtax and withholding in order, plus clearing, transport and port charges. VAT and withholding are shown separately because they can be claimed back.

Can it follow letters of credit?

Yes. Each LC is tracked from opening to settlement, with its fees, amendments and expiry date.

Does it keep books in foreign currency?

Yes. Purchases are recorded in the supplier's currency and in Birr, with exchange gains and losses per shipment.
